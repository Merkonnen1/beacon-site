Proving yourself into exhaustion

The fear of being found lacking shows up before the feedback meeting, in the emails you couldn't leave alone, in the hours spent on a deck that was already finished. Each time the answer is the same — do more, go longer — until the work stops and the restlessness doesn't.

An illustration, not a real person's journal — but the shape is real. Beacon draws only on your own entries, and quotes them back as the evidence for anything it says.

  1. From the first entry

    It starts building.

    Every recording is broken into what you talked about, how you talked about it, and what you kept circling without naming. Not summarised back at you — held as evidence.

  2. Across the first month

    It names the thing.

    The patterns get undeniable. Beacon stops you in your tracks, naming the exact fears and motivations you've been hiding from yourself — and shows you the entries that prove it.

  3. From there on

    It gets harder to hide from.

    Different kinds of pattern come into range at different depths, and the ones it has already found keep getting more certain. The longer you keep speaking, the more there is to see.

It doesn't guess early. It tells you what it's certain of — and certainty compounds.

Private by design

Built to be the least social app on your phone.

  • Your voice isn't stored

    Recordings are transcribed and discarded in the same request. What's kept is the text, not the sound of you saying it.

  • There's no feed to appear in

    Nothing to post, nobody to notify, no one else's entries to measure yourself against. There is no version of Beacon other people can see.

  • Deleting means deleted

    One action in Settings removes your account, every entry, and every pattern derived from them. It's a deletion, not a hide.

The full detail — what's collected, who processes it, how long it's kept — is in the Privacy Policy.
