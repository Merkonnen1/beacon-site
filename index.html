How it works

Three things, then nothing else to do.

01

Speak

Hold the orb and talk. No prompts to answer, no one on the other end, nothing to perform.

02

Keep

Your words are transcribed and filed by day. The recording itself is discarded — what's kept is the text.

03

Notice

When something has genuinely repeated, Beacon names it — grounded in your own entries, never in a guess.

What it notices

A week of small things. One thing underneath.

Beacon doesn't summarise your day back to you. It waits until something has actually repeated, then says the quiet part plainly.

Monday

"Skipped the gym again. Told myself I'd go tomorrow."

Wednesday

"He said the project looked good. I keep thinking about the parts that aren't."

Thursday

"Three hours on a deck that was fine after twenty minutes."

Saturday

"Couldn't get my head to go quiet tonight."

Beacon noticed

Proving yourself into exhaustion

The fear of being found lacking shows up before the feedback meeting, in the emails you couldn't leave alone, in the hours spent on a deck that was already finished. Each time the answer is the same — do more, go longer — until the work stops and the restlessness doesn't.

An illustration, not a real person's journal. Beacon draws only on your own entries, quotes them back as the evidence, and stays quiet when there isn't enough to be sure.

Private by design

Built to be the least social app on your phone.

  • Your voice isn't stored

    Recordings are transcribed and discarded in the same request. What's kept is the text, not the sound of you saying it.

  • There's no feed to appear in

    Nothing to post, nobody to notify, no one else's entries to measure yourself against. There is no version of Beacon other people can see.

  • Deleting means deleted

    One action in Settings removes your account, every entry, and every pattern derived from them. It's a deletion, not a hide.

The full detail — what's collected, who processes it, how long it's kept — is in the Privacy Policy.
